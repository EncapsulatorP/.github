## The Pirate Chest quest

[![The Pirate Chest quest: the P symbol, then four stops](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/assets/quest-header.svg)](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/treasure-hunt/quest/)

**P is the quest symbol.** *p* and *q* are the primes, **P** is what can be solved fast, and **?** is everything still unknown.

**Pick a treasure:** [① The two-factor gate](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/evidence/) · [② Goldbach observatory](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/#goldbach) · [③ The flat tangent](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/treasure-hunt/quest/) · ④ The defect you can count

<details>
<summary><b>Quest 1 · The two-factor gate</b> — try m = 12 before you open</summary>

The gate opens only for a centre *m* whose two neighbours are both prime. Count the prime factors of m² − 1, with multiplicity.

For m = 12: 12² − 1 = 143 = 11 × 13. Two factors, so the gate opens.

**Treasure (exact):** for m ≥ 3, Ω(m² − 1) = 2 exactly when m − 1 and m + 1 are both prime. Checked for 3 ≤ m < 200,000, which holds 2,160 twin centres. This restates the twin-prime condition; it does not show there are infinitely many.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Quest 2 · Goldbach observatory</b> — what is p · q when p + q = 100?</summary>

Every prime pair has a centre *m* and a half-gap *d*: p = m − d and q = m + d, so p · q = m² − d².

For 100 = 47 + 53: m = 50, d = 3, and 47 × 53 = 2491 = 50² − 3².

100 has six such pairs, all centred on 50: 3 + 97, 11 + 89, 17 + 83, 29 + 71, 41 + 59, 47 + 53.

**Treasure (exact identity).** Goldbach's claim for every even number is still open.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Quest 3 · The flat tangent hides a cubic</b> — four steps that almost bring you back</summary>

Take the logistic map x → r·x(1 − x) at r = 1 + √6 and stand next to a point *p* of its two-cycle. Four steps later you have moved by

f∘⁴(p + h) − (p + h) = −K·h³ + O(h⁴), with K = 20·r²·(2 ± √2) > 0 at the two points.

**Treasure (exact, local):** the tangent is flat, but the cubic that survives pulls back, so n·hₙ² → 1/(2K). [Fly the quest →](https://encapsulatorp.github.io/treasure-hunt/quest/)
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Quest 4 · The defect you can count</b> — which value does R(z) = (z² − 1)/(z² + 1) never reach?</summary>

Solve R(z) = a: z² = (1 + a)/(1 − a).

- a = 1 has no solution in the plane, so the value is missed: Θ(1) = 1.
- a = −1 has the single doubled solution z = 0: Θ(−1) = ½.
- every other a has two distinct solutions: Θ(a) = 0.

Here Θ(a) = 1 − lim N̄(r, a)/T(r, R) counts distinct solutions, and T(r, R) = 2·log r + O(1).

**Treasure (exact):** the total is 1 + ½ = 3/2, inside the bound ΣΘ(a) ≤ 2 of Nevanlinna's second fundamental theorem (R. Nevanlinna, 1925). The theorem is his; the worked example is elementary.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>A decoy chest</b> — "μ = +1 means twin primes"</summary>

Try m = 34: 34² − 1 = 1155 = 3 × 5 × 7 × 11, so μ(1155) = +1. But 33 = 3 × 11 and 35 = 5 × 7 are both composite.

**Counterexample:** the sign alone cannot certify twins. Below 200,000 it is +1 at 2,160 twin centres and at 30,215 centres that are not twins. The next decoys after 34 are 56, 86, 92, 94 and 104.
</details>

**Still unknown:** the bound that would settle Goldbach for every even number, P ≟ NP, and whether any function has a defect equal to the Brun deficit seen in the sign-flipping towers. No such construction is known.
